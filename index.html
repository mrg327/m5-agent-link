01 Colour means state

What the watch shows

Every session is an arc on the rim; the one you picked fills the middle. Colour says its state, and shape and motion say it again for anyone who can’t tell the colours apart. The amber and blue marks by the keys are the one exception. Pick a state or a part of the screen to see it on the watch.

04 From hook to wrist

How data reaches the watch

An agent does something and runs a hook: a small program it calls on every event. The hook tells agentlinkd, the daemon (a background program) on your PC, which turns the event into a few bytes and sends them over whichever link is up. Watch the beads; click one to open the packet.

05

Setup

Coding agents stop and wait for you in terminals you are not looking at. AgentLink puts every session on the watch, buzzes when one needs you, and lets you answer or jump to it.

What you need

  • An M5Stack StopWatch.
  • A PC on macOS, Linux or Windows.
  • Claude Code, GitHub Copilot CLI or pi.
  • Rust and ESP-IDF 5.5, to build the daemon and the firmware.

Three steps

  1. Flash the watch once over USB: idf.py -C firmware build flash.
  2. Run the daemon: cargo run -p agentlinkd in daemon/ (on macOS, daemon/tools/launchd.sh install starts it at login).
  3. Add the hooks to your agent: agentlink hooks install claude (or copilot, pi).

Desk or wrist

Desk: plug it in over USB-C and stand it on its magnetic back. The screen stays on for 10 minutes after you last touch it, then sleeps so the AMOLED does not burn in.

Wrist: on battery over Bluetooth, with your own band. The first time, the watch shows a six-digit passkey and your PC asks for it; after that they reconnect on their own.

The keys

  • A, yellow, upper left: bring the session’s terminal to the front. Twice: jump to the next one that needs you.
  • B, blue, upper right: next session. Hold: previous.
  • Power, red, lower left. USB-C at the bottom.
  • Lost? Hold A and B together to go back to the ring, or swipe right to go back one screen.

What reaches the watch

Only your PC talks to it: over USB, or Bluetooth with the passkey. There is no server and no account. The daemon serves only watches it trusts: the one on USB and the first one paired over Bluetooth; add more with agentlink devices trust.

The watch gets each session’s agent, project, branch and state, a one-line preview of its current step, the request you are asked to answer, and the agent’s last message when you open it.

Nothing is approved by accident

A tap never answers a request, and key A never approves. Approving is a 1.2 s hold on the request screen, denying a 0.8 s hold. The daemon takes an answer only from a trusted watch and only while that request is still open, and logs it in ~/.agentlink/audit.jsonl.

If you do not answer within 60 s, the terminal asks you as usual.

Full setup in the README: macOS permissions, kitty, terminal jobs, gauges.

06 Your own data

Session replay

The daemon keeps a trace of every session. Load one and the watch above plays it back, sped up, so you can see what it showed and when it buzzed. The file never leaves your browser.
